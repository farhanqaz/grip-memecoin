Supply follows grip.

Lock 60% of the live float and the token mints, paid to the lockers. Let it fall under 35% and it burns, out of a reserve the contract owns. No owner mint, no pause, no setters.

Public demo instance. The locked share above is one scripted account, renewed every 10 minutes by a keeper, not organic demand. An epoch here is 60 seconds, not the 86,400 a production deployment would use, so supply moves about 1,440x faster than it would in the wild. The rule is unchanged: no admin path exists, lock() and tick() are public methods.

The rule

Four lines, fixed at deploy

01

Locks expire

A lock stops counting at its deadline, before the rule runs. Grip has to be renewed, or it decays into a burn epoch. One passive holder cannot hold the metric up forever.

until_epoch ≤ epoch
02

Grip at 60% mints 1%

Paid pro rata to whoever is locked, and paid out unlocked. The locked total always equals the sum of positions, so a reward never inflates the principal that earned it. The mint stops at 4× genesis: a percentage of the current supply compounds, and a rule with no ceiling is a runaway by construction.

mint_bps = 100
03

Grip at 35% or less burns 2%

Drawn only from the reserve the contract holds. When the reserve is spent, the epoch records hold instead. The burn budget is finite by construction.

burn_bps = 200
04

Anyone can tick

One call evaluates the epoch. There is no admin key, no pause, and no upgrade path: after deploy, the deployer has no power over the numbers.
